THE PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND 
Introduction 
Disease surveillance is defined as the systematic and continuous collection, analysis, 
interpretation, and dissemination of health data essential for planning, implementing, and 
evaluating public health practice (World Health Organization [WHO], 2018). Eff ective 
surveillance systems enable health authorities to detect outbreaks early, guide targeted 
interventions, and allocate resources based on spatial and temporal patterns of disease 
occurrence. Globally, the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) an d machine learning into 
public health surveillance has significantly expanded the analytical capabilities available to 
health agencies, enabling automated outbreak detection, predictive forecasting, and data -
driven decision support that traditional manual methods cannot provide (Srivastava et al., 
2024). 
In the Philippines, the Department of Health (DOH) implements the Philippine 
Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (PIDSR) system as the national framework for 
monitoring notifiable diseases (DOH, 2014). While PIDSR provides a standardized reporting  
structure through line listings and tabular aggregation, its implementation at the city 
epidemiology level remains largely spreadsheet-based and limited in spatial visualization and 
automated outbreak detection capability. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention 
(CDC, 2019) has emphasized that electronic and digital surveillance systems significantly 
improve timeliness, completeness, and response efficiency, highlighting the operational gap 
that persists in many local government health offices.

Parañaque City, as one of the highly urbanized cities in Metro Manila, Philippines, 
generates substantial weekly disease data through its network of health centers, hospitals, and 
sentinel sites. The City Epidemiology Section Unit (CESU) is responsible for  monitoring 
disease trends and recommending public health responses. Despite maintaining disease records

Scope and Limitations of the Study 
The study covers the design, development, testing, and evaluation of H -MAP: A Heat 
Map Disease Surveillance and Outbreak Prediction System for the City Epidemiology Section 
Unit of Parañaque City. The proposed system will be deployed as a path-based web application 
at hrmo.paranaque.gov.ph/hmap on the existing DICT -provisioned server used by the

provided by the City Epidemiology Section Unit, covering the years 2010 to  2023 and 
comprising over 30,000 patient -level records across 16 barangays and multiple notifiable

The scope of H -MAP includes five functional modules: an HRMO -integrated 
authentication module; a data digitization and records management module for encoding 
PIDSR-compliant case data; an interactive heat map visualization module built with Leaflet.js; 
a disease trend monitoring module with automated WHO EWARN epidemic threshold alerting 
(WHO, 2018); and an AI machine learning prediction module implemented as an internal 
Python Flask microservice delivering Prophet-based weekly case forecasts and Random Forest 
barangay risk classifications, consistent with methodologies validated in the Philippine context 
by Carvajal et al. (2018) and Olana et al. (2025).

management, or biometric data processing. The AI machine learning outputs of H -MAP are 
intended as decision-support indicators and do not replace the professional judgment of trained 
epidemiologists. The system is limited to the 16 barangays of Parañaque City and does not 
include cases from adjacent cities recorded in the dataset. Evaluation will be conducted using 
an ISO/IEC 25010-based questionnaire administered to purposively selected health official and

Disease Surveillance and the PIDSR Framework 
Disease surveillance is defined as the systematic and continuous collection, analysis, 
interpretation, and dissemination of health data essential for planning, implementing, and 
evaluating public health practice (WHO, 2018). Surveillance systems enable early detection of 
outbreaks and guide public health interventions. The WHO (2018) identifies disease 
surveillance as a core function of national health systems and emphasizes that strengthening 
surveillance infrastructure requires both improved reporting acc uracy and enhanced analytic 
capacity. 
Surveillance systems are commonly classified into four types: passive, active, sentinel, 
and syndromic. Passive surveillance depends on routine reporting from health facilities, while 
active surveillance involves proactive case identification by health aut horities. Sentinel 
surveillance monitors selected reporting sites that serve as representative indicators of disease 
trends, and syndromic surveillance detects patterns based on symptoms before laboratory 
===== PAGE 12 =====
12 
 
confirmation is obtained (WHO, 2018). Although passive surveillance remains widely used in 
developing countries including the Philippines, limitations in timeliness and spatial 
interpretation persist as recognized challenges. The CDC (2019) has emphasized that 
enhancing passive systems with digital data management and geospatial visualization 
improves detection of outbreak clusters and transmission patterns. 
In the Philippines, the DOH implements the PIDSR system as the national disease 
notification and reporting framework. Under PIDSR, health facilities are required to report 
cases of 28 priority diseases using standardized case report forms that include dise ase 
classification, case outcome, patient demographics, reporting facility, and barangay -level 
geographic location (DOH, 2014). While PIDSR ensures uniform data collection, the system 
does not inherently provide advanced geospatial analytics or automated s tatistical outbreak 
detection at the city epidemiology level. H -MAP is designed as a supplementary analytical 
layer on top of existing PIDSR data, enhancing the local surveillance capabilities of the CESU 
without replacing or duplicating the national reporting workflow. 
Geographic Information Systems in Public Health 
Geographic Information Systems are computer -based platforms designed to capture, 
store, analyze, and visualize geographically referenced data. In public health, GIS has become 
a powerful tool for mapping disease distribution and identifying spatial relatio nships among 
cases (Nykiforuk & Flaman, 2011). Nykiforuk and Flaman (2011) conducted a comprehensive 
review of GIS applications in health promotion and found that GIS -based approaches 
consistently provided superior capacity for spatial disease visualization compared to traditional 
tabular analysis, enabling more targeted and efficient public health interventions. Lawson 
(2018) similarly explains that spatial epidemiology uses GIS tools to detect clustering patterns 
===== PAGE 13 =====
13 
 
and assess environmental risk factors, transforming raw epidemiological data into actionable 
intelligence that enables faster and more informed decisions by health authorities. 
The emergence of open -source and web -based GIS technologies has substantially 
reduced the barrier to adoption for local health agencies. Boulos et al. (2011) reviewed web -
based GIS technologies in public health and found that open -source web mapping platfo rms 
provide sufficient functionality for disease mapping tasks previously requiring proprietary 
desktop GIS software, with ease of deployment, cross -platform accessibility, and zero 
licensing costs identified as key practical advantages for health agencies  in resource -
constrained settings. Leaflet.js, the open-source JavaScript library for interactive web mapping 
selected for H -MAP, renders maps directly in the browser without requiring client -side 
software installation, which is a practical advantage in he alth office environments where 
workstation configurations vary. 
Geographic base map data for H -MAP will be sourced from OpenStreetMap, and 
barangay boundary data will be derived from the Philippine GIS Data Exchange Center 
(PhilGIS) under an open data license. This approach avoids proprietary licensing costs while 
delivering the spatial reference framework needed for barangay -level disease case geocoding 
and heat map generation, consistent with DICT's open-source government systems policy. 
Heat Mapping and Spatial Cluster Detection 
Heat maps represent disease case density using color gradients to visualize intensity 
distribution across a geographic area. Areas with high concentrations of cases appear in 
warmer colors, while lower concentrations appear cooler, enhancing situational awareness by 
enabling immediate recognition of outbreak hotspots (Lawson, 2018). Spatial visualization of 
===== PAGE 14 =====
14 
 
this type enhances public health planning while minimizing individual -level exposure of 
patient information, particularly when aggregated to the barangay or district level. 
Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) is the primary spatial statistical method underlying 
heat map generation. KDE calculates a smoothed estimate of case density at each point in a 
geographic space based on the distribution of reported cases, producing a continuous, visually 
intuitive surface of disease concentration (Lawson, 2018). Spatial autocorrelation methods 
complement KDE by identifying whether the clustering of cases is statistically significant 
beyond what would be expected by chance. These cluster dete ction methods strengthen 
outbreak detection by revealing patterns that may not be evident in tabular data, thereby 
supporting earlier intervention strategies (Lawson, 2018). 
Dong et al. (2020) demonstrated the wide -scale operational effectiveness of spatial 
visualization during the COVID -19 pandemic. Their development of the Johns Hopkins 
University COVID-19 Dashboard, which integrated real -time GIS mapping with case count 
data, showed that geospatial visualization in a web-based format significantly improved global 
situational awareness and informed public health decision -making. This system serves as an 
internationally recognized benchmark for the dashboard design approach adopted in H-MAP, 
particularly the integration of map -based visualization with tabular and chart -based data 
summaries. 
Epidemic Threshold Methods and Automated Outbreak Detection 
Outbreak detection mechanisms in epidemiological surveillance commonly rely on 
statistical aberration detection methods that compare current case counts against historical 
baselines. The most widely used approach, consistent with the WHO Early Warning and 
Response Network (EWARN) methodology, defines the epidemic threshold as the mean 
===== PAGE 15 =====
15 
 
number of cases recorded during the same epidemiological week across the previous five years, 
plus two standard deviations (WHO, 2018). When current case counts exceed this value, an 
outbreak warning is generated. This can be expressed as: 
                 Alert if: Current Cases > Baseline Mean + 2 (Standard Deviation) 
This threshold model is epidemiologically principled because it accounts for both the 
expected seasonal level of disease activity and the year-to-year variability in case counts. The 
same mathematical basis is used in the Philippines' own national PIDSR five-year average alert 
tables, prepared annually by the National Epidemiology Center (DOH, 2014). The CDC 
developed the Early Aberration Reporting System (EARS), which utilizes moving averages 
and standard deviation thresholds to identify abnormal disease a ctivity (CDC, 2019). Time -
series moving averages smooth temporal fluctuations in case counts and detect aberrations, 
expressed as: 
                 MA(t) = [C(t-1) + C(t-2) + C(t-3)] / 3 
Where C(t) represents case counts at time t. These threshold approaches are widely 
accepted in surveillance systems because they balance simplicity, interpretability, and early 
detection capability (CDC, 2019; WHO, 2018). H -MAP will automate this threshold  
calculation for all monitored diseases using the 14 -year historical PIDSR dataset available 
from the Parañaque City CESU. 
Artificial Intelligence in Disease Surveillance 
Artificial intelligence has emerged as a transformative capability in disease 
surveillance, enabling health systems to move beyond passive data collection toward 
automated detection, prediction, and decision support. Srivastava et al. (2024) documented the 
expanding role of AI in early diagnosis and detection of infectious diseases, noting that AI -
===== PAGE 16 =====
16 
 
assisted surveillance systems analyze historical data patterns, generate automated alerts, and 
support clinical and public health decision -making in ways that manual surveillance cannot 
match. Their review emphasized that AI's capacity to identify subtle p atterns across large 
historical datasets makes it particularly valuable in settings where disease data has been 
collected over many years but has not been systematically analyzed for predictive purposes. 
The WHO (2018) and CDC (2019) similarly acknowledge that digital and AI -enabled 
surveillance systems significantly improve timeliness and detection accuracy over traditional 
methods. The WHO (2018) highlights that early warning systems incorporating automa ted 
analytical models reduce the time between outbreak onset and health authority notification, 
enabling earlier and more effective public health response. The CDC (2019) has actively 
developed AI-assisted surveillance tools including the Early Aberration Reporting System, 
which operationalizes statistical learning principles for automated anomaly detection in disease 
incidence data, demonstrating the practical value of embedding AI within routine surveillance 
workflows. 
For H -MAP, AI is embedded in two specific components: the Prophet time -series 
forecasting model, which applies additive regression and automatic seasonality detection to 
generate four-week-ahead disease case predictions, and the Random Forest classifier, w hich 
uses ensemble learning to classify each of Parañaque City's 16 barangays by disease risk level 
for a given week. Together, these components constitute H -MAP's AI-assisted early warning 
capability, providing health officials with forward -looking intelligence that complements the 
automated epidemic threshold alerts. This design reflects the principle identified by Srivastava 
et al. (2024) that AI in surveillance should serve as a decision -support layer rather than a 
replacement for epidemiological expertise.

Machine Learning for Disease Forecasting: Prophet Time-Series Models 
Predictive modeling applies statistical and time -series techniques to forecast disease 
trends using historical incidence data. Shaman and Karspeck (2012) demonstrated that 
influenza outbreaks can be forecasted using time -series modeling and statistical inf erence 
techniques, establishing the foundational evidence that machine learning-based forecasting can 
provide operationally useful lead time for public health preparedness. Prophet, developed by 
Meta's Core Data Science team and released as open-source software, decomposes time-series 
data into trend, yearly seasonality, weekly seasonality, and holiday effect components, fitting 
these through an additive regression model that is robust to missing data and irregular reporting 
patterns (Taylor & Letham, 2018). 
Olana et al. (2025) compared SARIMA, Neural Network Autoregression, Random 
Forest, LSTM, TBATS, and Prophet for dengue forecasting at the national level in the 
Philippines using monthly data from 2017 to 2024. Their study confirmed the strong 
seasonality of Philippine dengue incidence and demonstrated that time-series models including 
Prophet can produce reliable forecasts when trained on sufficiently long historical data, 
providing the most current evidence in the Philippine literature for the forecasting approach 
employed in H -MAP. Chakraborty et al. (2019) similarly applied Prophet to dengue 
forecasting and reported that the model's automated seasonality handling produced forecasts 
with substantially lower error rates than traditional ARIMA models, partic ularly for diseases 
with strong and regular seasonal patterns. These findings directly validate the Prophet 
forecasting component of H-MAP, which will target a four-week forecast horizon for Dengue 
and other priority diseases exhibiting consistent seasonal  behavior in the Parañaque City 
PIDSR dataset.

It is important to note that both Olana et al. (2025) and Chakraborty et al. (2019) 
produced standalone forecasting models without integration into operational dashboard

within an authenticated, role -based web dashboard accessible to city epidemiology unit staff 
without requiring them to operate machine learning tools directly. 
Machine Learning for Barangay Risk Classification: Random Forest 
Random Forest is an ensemble machine learning method that constructs multiple 
decision trees during training and outputs the class predicted by the majority of trees (Breiman, 
2001). It is valued in classification tasks for its robustness to overfitting, i ts ability to handle 
mixed feature types, and its capacity to capture complex interactions between features. In the 
Philippine context, Carvajal et al. (2018) applied Random Forest, alongside General Additive 
Modeling, SARIMA, and Gradient Boosting, to for ecast dengue incidence in Metropolitan 
Manila using meteorological and historical case data. Their study found that Random Forest 
achieved competitive predictive accuracy and identified temporal patterns that single -
technique models could not reliably capture, providing direct local evidence for the suitability 
of Random Forest in Philippine dengue surveillance contexts. 
Shi et al. (2016) demonstrated that ensemble learning methods outperformed logistic 
regression and single decision tree approaches in classifying high-risk geographic units based 
on historical case patterns and seasonal features in a dengue surveillance context. For H-MAP, 
the Random Forest classifier will be trained on 14 years of Parañaque City PIDSR data to 
predict Low, Moderate, or High disease risk for each of the city's 16 barangays for a given 
week and disease, using features including barangay identity, morbidity week, calendar month, 
recent case counts, prior-year case counts, and the five-year historical mean. The combination 
===== PAGE 19 =====
19 
 
of epidemic threshold alerting at the city level with Random Forest risk classification at the 
barangay level provides a layered analytical design that addresses both outbreak detection and 
geographic targeting of public health resources. 
As with the Prophet forecasting component, the Random Forest classifier in H -MAP 
addresses the operational gap identified in the local literature: Carvajal et al. (2018) produced 
a machine learning model for Metro Manila dengue forecasting but did not inte grate it into a 
platform usable by health officers in routine surveillance operations. H -MAP converts the 
classifier's outputs into a color-coded barangay risk overlay on the Leaflet.js map, making AI-
generated risk assessments directly interpretable by health personnel without machine learning 
expertise. 
PHP-Based Web Systems and HRMO Portal Integration 
PHP is a widely used server -side scripting language specifically designed for web 
development, supporting dynamic content generation, session management, database 
connectivity, and form handling. Its adoption in Philippine government web systems is well 
established, with many LGU portals, eServices platforms, and information management 
systems implemented in PHP due to its open-source licensing and broad server compatibility. 
The Parañaque City HRMO portal at hrmo.paranaque.gov.ph is implemented in PHP, making 
PHP the natural choice for H-MAP to ensure compatibility with the existing codebase, session 
infrastructure, and database configuration of the host environment. 
Session-based authentication in PHP provides a practical mechanism for integrating H-
MAP with the HRMO portal's access control system. When an employee logs into the HRMO 
portal, PHP creates a server -side session storing the employee's identifier, name, ro le, and 
employment status. H-MAP will access this session data directly, verifying that the requesting

user is both authenticated in the HRMO portal and recorded as an active employee in the 
HRMO MySQL database before granting access to disease surveillance features. This 
approach eliminates the need for a separate H -MAP user database, reduces administrativ e

records, including immediate revocation of access upon HR recording of an employee

From a deployment perspective, implementing H -MAP as a path -based directory at 
hrmo.paranaque.gov.ph/hmap avoids the need for new DNS configuration, additional SSL 
certificate provisioning, or separate server allocation. The proposed system will share the 
existing server resources, PHP runtime, and MySQL instance of the HRMO portal, minimizing 
infrastructure requirements and simplifying both initial deployment and ongoing maintenance.

Role-Based Access Control and Data Privacy in Health Systems 
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) is a security model that restricts system access 
based on the roles assigned to individual users, ensuring that each user can only access 
functions and data appropriate to their responsibilities. In health information syste ms, RBAC 
is a foundational security requirement because such systems process sensitive personal 
information including patient identities, addresses, disease diagnoses, and outcome data. 
Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012, requires that he alth information 
systems implement access controls that limit data exposure to personnel with a legitimate need 
for the information (Republic Act No. 10173, 2012). The National Privacy Commission (n.d.) 
has issued guidelines emphasizing that personal data collected for public health purposes must 
be protected through technical and organizational measures appropriate to the risk. 
H-MAP will implement RBAC through a two -layer access control architecture. The 
first layer will be provided by the HRMO portal authentication, which verifies that the

Synthesis of the Literature and Research Gap 
The reviewed literature establishes several converging conclusions that collectively 
support the design and development of H -MAP. First, disease surveillance systems require 
both structured data collection and enhanced analytic capacity, and digital platfo rms 
significantly improve both dimensions compared to manual, spreadsheet -based approaches 
(WHO, 2018; CDC, 2019). Second, open-source web GIS technologies, particularly Leaflet.js 
with OpenStreetMap, have reached functional maturity sufficient for profess ional-grade 
disease surveillance mapping without proprietary licensing costs (Boulos et al., 2011; 
Nykiforuk & Flaman, 2011). Third, the WHO EWARN epidemic threshold methodology and 
CDC EARS provide statistically principled, operationally established standards for automated 
outbreak detection that are directly compatible with PIDSR-formatted data (WHO, 2018; CDC, 
2019; DOH, 2014). 
Fourth, AI machine learning models including Prophet and Random Forest have 
demonstrated reliable performance in communicable disease forecasting and spatial risk 
classification in the Philippine context specifically. Carvajal et al. (2018) demonstrated th at 
Random Forest achieves competitive accuracy for dengue forecasting in Metropolitan Manila. 
Olana et al. (2025), in the most current Philippine study, confirmed that Prophet and Random 
Forest are among the viable models for national -scale dengue forecast ing using PIDSR -
formatted data spanning 2017 to 2024. These two studies provide the strongest available local 
validation for the AI components of H -MAP. Fifth, Srivastava et al. (2024) established the 
broader conceptual basis for AI -assisted disease surveillance, confirming that embedding AI 
===== PAGE 28 =====
28 
 
within operational health information systems improves detection speed and decision-support 
quality across diverse health contexts. Sixth, Republic Act No. 10173 (2012) and National 
Privacy Commission guidelines establish data privacy and RBAC as non -negotiable 
requirements for health information systems processing personal disease data. 
Despite these converging foundations, the reviewed literature reveals a specific and 
addressable research gap. Carvajal et al. (2018) and Olana et al. (2025), the two most relevant 
Philippine machine learning studies, both produced standalone forecasting models focused on 
predictive accuracy without embedding their outputs into operational web -based surveillance 
platforms. Neither study addressed the practical challenge of making AI -generated disease 
forecasts accessible to non -technical health personnel at  the city epidemiology unit level. 
Existing LGU dashboards in Metro Manila use general-purpose tools without GIS or machine 
learning capability, and national DOH systems do not provide city -level spatial visualization 
or AI-assisted forecasting. H-MAP addresses this specific intersection of gaps by developing 
an authenticated, role-based, web-based platform that embeds AI-generated Prophet forecasts 
and Random Forest risk classifications within an accessible surveillance dashboard designed 
specifically for CESU personnel using 14 years of Parañaque City PIDSR data. The system's 
deployment within the HRMO portal infrastructure further addresses the practical access 
control requirement absent from all reviewed systems.

Project Design 
This study will employ an Applied Developmental Research Design, in which the 
primary objective is the design, construction, testing, and formal evaluation of a functional 
software system that addresses a documented operational need within a specific insti tutional 
context. Applied developmental research is appropriate here because the contribution of the 
study is the H -MAP system itself, together with evidence of its quality based on ISO/IEC 
25010 evaluation criteria (ISO, 2011). The study does not aim prim arily to generate new 
theoretical knowledge but to produce and formally evaluate a practical artifact of direct 
operational value to the City Epidemiology Section Unit, specifically addressing the 
operational gap identified in the Philippine machine learning literature by Carvajal et al. (2018) 
and Olana et al. (2025). 
Development will follow an Agile -Inspired Iterative Prototyping Model, with the 
project progressing through repeated cycles of planning, development, testing, and feedback -
driven refinement within a one-month development timeline. This focused scope is intentional:

rather than pursuing feature breadth, H -MAP prioritizes the delivery of five well -tested 
modules that collectively address the core surveillance needs of the CESU. The iterative 
approach is appropriate because the HRMO authentication integration, AI machin e learning 
microservice communication, and heat map parameter calibration each require short cycles of 
testing and adjustment that benefit from incremental rather than linear development. 
The complete development lifecycle will consist of the following five phases: 
a. Requirements Analysis: structured consultation with CESU health officials to

c. Development: iterative construction of all five H -MAP modules and AI model

d. Testing: functional, reliability, performance, and usability testing using 
documented test cases across all modules. 
e. Evaluation: ISO/IEC 25010 -based questionnaire administration to CESU health

authentication. When an employee accesses hrmo.paranaque.gov.ph/hmap, the H -MAP PHP

System Modules 
H-MAP will be composed of five functional modules, scoped for completion within 
the one-month development timeline: 
Module 1: Data Digitization and Records Management. This module will provide a 
structured web -based interface for encoding PIDSR disease case records into the H -MAP 
MySQL database. Input fields will mirror the PIDSR case report form, consistent with DOH 
(2014) reporting standards, including disease name, case classification, date of onset, date of 
admission, patient barangay, reporting health facility, age, sex, vaccination history, and case 
outcome. Input validation will check completeness and classification consistency before record 
insertion. A batch import function will support CSV upload of multiple records using a PIDSR-
compatible template, facilitating transfer of historical data from existing Excel -based 
surveillance records. 
Module 2: Heat Map Visualization. This module will render an interactive disease 
surveillance map using Leaflet.js with OpenStreetMap tile layers. Upon selecting a disease and 
date range, the module will retrieve geocoded case records from MySQL and pass them to the 
Leaflet.heat plugin, which will compute and render the KDE heat map as a color gradient 
overlay on the base map (Lawson, 2018). A GeoJSON barangay boundary layer will be 
rendered as a semi -transparent polygon overlay, with each barangay polygon colored Low 
(green), Moderate (yellow), or High (red) based on the current output of the AI Random Forest 
risk classifier retrieved from the Python machine learning microservice (Breiman, 2001; 
Carvajal et al., 2018). 
Module 3: Disease Trend Monitoring and Epidemic Threshold Alert. This module will 
provide the core epidemiological surveillance dashboard. For the selected disease, the module

will query the H -MAP MySQL database to retrieve weekly case counts for the current year 
and compare them against pre-computed threshold values. Threshold values will be computed

with WHO EWARN methodology (WHO, 2018) and CDC EARS (CDC, 2019). Results will 
be displayed as a Chart.js line chart overlaying the current year epidemic curve against the 
historical mean and threshold. A color -coded status indicator will update automatically each

the threshold, and red if the threshold is exceeded.

map. A visible note on the prediction panel w ill state that AI machine learning outputs are

AI Python Machine Learning Microservice 
The AI machine learning component of H-MAP will run as a Flask application on the 
same DICT server, listening on internal port 5001 and accessible only through localhost, not 
exposed to the public internet. The microservice will provide two endpoints: /pre dict/forecast 
for Prophet model execution, and /predict/risk for Random Forest classification. Both models 
will be pre-trained and loaded at startup for fast response without retraining on each request. 
Prophet Model. One Prophet model will be trained per priority disease using the 
complete aggregated weekly case time -series from 2010 to 2023 (Taylor & Letham, 2018). 
The training process follows the approach validated by Olana et al. (2025) for Philippine  
dengue data, aggregating case records by disease and morbidity week into the two -column 
dataframe format required by Prophet (ds for week start date, y for case count). Each trained 
model will be serialized using Python's pickle module and stored on the server. Administrators 
may trigger model retraining through the H-MAP Administrator panel as new surveillance data 
is added. 
Random Forest Model. The Random Forest classifier will be trained using labeled 
historical observations derived from the PIDSR dataset at the disease -barangay-week level, 
following the approach employed by Carvajal et al. (2018) for Metro Manila dengue dat a 
(Breiman, 2001). Each observation will be labeled High risk if the barangay's case count 
exceeded the epidemic threshold for that week, Moderate if between the historical mean and 
threshold, and Low if at or below the mean. The feature vector will include barangay identifier, 
===== PAGE 41 =====
41 
 
morbidity week number, calendar month, current week case count, same-week-prior-year case 
count, five-year historical mean, ratio of current to historical mean, and cumulative year -to-
date case count for the barangay. A scikit -learn RandomForestClassifier with 200 estimators 
and class-weight balancing will be used. 
Model Validation. Both models will be validated using a temporal hold -out approach 
consistent with Olana et al. (2025): training on 2010 to 2020 data and evaluating against 2021 
to 2023 data. For the Prophet model, Mean Absolute Percentage Error will be re ported for 
forecast weeks. For the Random Forest classifier, accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score will 
be reported per risk class. Validation results will be documented in the system evaluation 
findings.

The relationships among the H -MAP database tables are governed by foreign key 
constraints implemented at the MySQL InnoDB engine level. The cases table is the central 
transaction table and holds foreign keys referencing the disease_id of the diseases table , the 
barangay_id of the barangays table, and the facility_id of the facilities table, establishing one-
to-many relationships in each instance. The facilities table additionally holds a foreign key 
referencing the barangays table, reflecting that each faci lity is located within one barangay. 
The thresholds table references the diseases table through a foreign key with a CASCADE 
delete rule, ensuring threshold records are automatically removed when a disease record is 

===== PAGE 50 =====
50 
 
deleted, and enforces a unique constraint on the combination of disease_id and 
morbidity_week to guarantee one threshold value per disease per epidemiological week. The 
ai_predictions table holds foreign keys to both the diseases table and the barangays table, with 
the barangay reference accepting NULL values to accommodate Prophet forecast records that 
are not barangay -specific. The user_roles table references the HRMO employee database 
through a logical cross-schema reference rather than an enforced foreign key constraint, as the 
two tables reside in separate database schemas — H-MAP's hmap_db and the HRMO portal's 
own database. Similarly, the entered_by field in the cases table and the employee_id field in 
the audit_log table reference the HRMO employee i dentifier through logical cross -schema 
references, with employee identity verified through the HRMO session at the point of data 
entry rather than through a database-level constraint.

Software Testing Plan 
A multi-level testing strategy will be applied across all H-MAP modules prior to formal 
evaluation. Testing will be organized as follows: 

===== PAGE 51 =====
51 
 
A. Functionality Testing. Functional test cases will be developed for each module and 
major system operation, specifying the module, operational procedure, expected result, and 
observed system response. Test cases will cover HRMO session detection and redi rect 
behavior; employment status verification; role -based access control enforcement; data entry 
validation and database insertion; heat map rendering for different disease and date range 
inputs; epidemic curve chart accuracy against WHO EWARN thresholds (WHO, 2018); Flask 
AI microservice communication from PHP; Prophet forecast JSON parsing and chart rendering 
(Taylor & Letham, 2018; Olana et al., 2025); and Random Forest risk classification map 
overlay accuracy (Breiman, 2001; Carvajal et al., 2018). 
B. Reliability Testing. Reliability testing will evaluate H -MAP's consistent 
performance under repeated use, including repeated execution of the full dashboard load 
sequence across multiple sessions and user role levels, and testing of AI machine learning 
prediction request handling under repeated calls. The system's behavior when the Flask 
microservice is temporarily unavailable will be verified to ensure that the PHP dashboard 
displays an informative message without failing. 
C. Performance Testing. Performance testing will measure response times for key 
operations: initial dashboard load time, heat map rendering time for datasets of varying case 
volume, Chart.js chart rendering time, and Flask AI machine learning prediction response time 
from PHP cURL call to JSON receipt. Target response times are set at under 3 seconds for 
dashboard load and under 5 seconds for AI prediction response, consistent with web 
application usability standards. 
D. Usability Testing. Usability testing will be conducted with selected CESU health 
official respondents who will perform standardized task sequences including logging in 
===== PAGE 52 =====
52 
 
through the HRMO portal, encoding a disease case record, navigating the heat map, 
interpreting the epidemic threshold alert, and accessing an AI disease forecast. Post -task 
questionnaire items will assess ease of navigation, clarity of map and chart visual izations, 
understandability of AI prediction outputs, and overall interface satisfaction, following ISO 
9241 principles. 
Evaluation Model 
The formal evaluation of H -MAP will use selected characteristics of the ISO/IEC 
25010 software product quality model (ISO, 2011) and an adopted software evaluation 
instrument. The selected quality characteristics are: 
a. Functionality: the degree to which H -MAP provides all intended disease 
surveillance, heat mapping, epidemic threshold alerting, and AI machine learning 
prediction features. 
b. Reliability: the degree to which H -MAP, including the PHP web application and 
the Python Flask AI microservice, performs its functions consistently under normal 
operational conditions. 
c. Usability: the degree to which CESU health officials and data encoders can use H-
MAP effectively and with satisfaction to accomplish surveillance tasks, assessed 
following ISO 9241 principles. 
d. Performance Efficiency: the degree to which H-MAP renders maps, charts, and AI 
machine learning predictions within acceptable response times under the DICT 
server's resource constraints. 
===== PAGE 53 =====
53 
 
e. Maintainability: the degree to which the PHP codebase and Python AI machine 
learning models can be modified and updated to accommodate new diseases or 
changed surveillance requirements (ISO, 2011). 
Respondents will be purposively selected from: 
a. 25 to 30 health officials, medical officers, epidemiologists, and data personnel from 
the City Epidemiology Section Unit and affiliated health centers and sentinel sites 
of Parañaque City. 
b. Five Information Technology professionals with expertise in web systems, 
government IT, or health informatics. 
The evaluation instrument will be a structured 5 -point Likert -scale questionnaire 
adapted from ISO/IEC 25010 criteria (ISO, 2011), rated as follows: 
a. 5: Outstanding / Strongly Agree 
b. 4: Very Acceptable / Agree 
c. 3: Acceptable / Neutral 
d. 2: Needs Improvement / Disagree 
e. 1: Poor / Strongly Disagree 
Data Processing and Statistical Treatment 
The following descriptive statistical methods will be applied to evaluation response 
data: 
a. Frequency Distribution: summarizing the distribution of respondent ratings across 
the five-point scale for each evaluation item. 
===== PAGE 54 =====
54 
 
b. Weighted Mean: computed as the sum of (rating x frequency) divided by total 
respondents for each item and each ISO/IEC 25010 quality characteristic, providing 
a single numerical indicator of overall acceptability (ISO, 2011). 
c. Standard Deviation: computed per item to measure variability in respondent ratings 
and indicate consensus or divergence of assessment. 
Weighted mean scores will be interpreted against the following acceptability scale: 
a. 4.21 to 5.00: Outstanding / Very Acceptable 
b. 3.41 to 4.20: Acceptable / Agreeable 
c. 2.61 to 3.40: Moderately Acceptable 
d. 1.81 to 2.60: Needs Improvement 
e. 1.00 to 1.80: Poor / Unacceptable 
Acceptance Criterion: H-MAP will be considered to have met the evaluation standard 
if the overall weighted mean across all five ISO/IEC 25010 quality characteristics is at least 
3.41. Inferential statistical analysis will not be conducted given the descrip tive intent of the 
evaluation phase and the sample size consistent with purposive sampling in applied 
developmental research. 
Security Considerations 
H-MAP's security architecture addresses the sensitivity of disease surveillance data 
under Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173, 2012), 
and the applicable guidelines of the National Privacy Commission (n.d.). The primary security 
mechanisms to be implemented are: 
===== PAGE 55 =====
55 
 
a. HRMO-Based Access Control: access restricted to active Parañaque City 
government employees through HRMO portal session verification and real -time 
employment status checking, with no public access permitted. 
b. Role-Based Feature Restriction: RBAC ensuring Data Encoders cannot access AI 
predictions or bulk exports; Health Analysts  cannot access system configuration; 
and all Administrator actions are logged, consistent with Republic Act No. 10173 
(2012). 
c. Internal AI Service Isolation: the Python Flask AI microservice bound to 
localhost:5001, accessible only from the PHP application on the same server, not 
exposed to any external network interface. 
d. Input Sanitization and Injection Prevention: all user -submitted data validated and 
sanitized using PHP prepared statements with parameterized queries via PDO. 
e. Audit Logging: all data entry, modification, deletion, export, and AI prediction 
access events recorded in the audit log with employee identifier, action type, 
timestamp, and IP address, consistent with Republic Act No. 10173 (2012). 
f. Data Minimization in Display: dashboard visualizations and AI machine learning 
outputs will operate on aggregated, barangay -level case counts without exposing 
individual patient identifiers, consistent with the data minimization principle under 
Republic Ac t No. 10173 (2012) and National Privacy Commission (n.d.) 
guidelines.
